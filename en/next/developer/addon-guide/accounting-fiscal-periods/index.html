    Fiscal periods and lock statuses

    Status: implemented. Design: ADR 0048 D3 R2 (period records), ADR 0147 D2, D7 and D8 (one calendar with an accounting and a stock status, reopening, the two-step locks). Behaviour: docs/specs/accounting/locking.md (SPEC-ACCLOCK-023, -099 to -103). Code: addons/account/src/fiscal/.

    The reference keeps lock dates on the company and compares every date with them. ERPForAll keeps those company dates for display and for their setters, and stores one account.fiscal.period row per company and month: the row’s statuses are what writers decide on, because only a row can be locked and drained safely (ADR 0147 D8). The stock addon never reads this table; stock asks through stock_account (ADR 0147 G5).

    Period rows

    Section titled “Period rows”
    ColumnMeaning
    company_id, fiscal_year, kind, numberunique together; fiscal_year is the year of the fiscal year’s last day; kind is regular (numbers 1 to 12) or adjustment (13 to 16, one day long, created by the year close)
    date_from, date_tothe range, both days included; regular periods of a company never overlap (an exclusion constraint)
    statusopen, closed (fiscal-year lock), hard_locking, hard_locked; a CHECK refuses any other value, and period_admission returns an internal error naming the period for a value it does not know (never Open)
    stock_lock_statusopen, stock_locking, stock_locked (a CHECK); a hard_locked period is always stock_locked (a CHECK)
    *_started_by, *_started_atset exactly while the matching lock is in progress (a CHECK)
    stock_lock_generation, hard_lock_generationrun counters of the two locks
    closed_at, hard_locked_atwhen the period left open, when it reached hard_locked

    The record API refuses create, write and delete, also with elevated rights; the readonly accounting group reads the periods of its companies. No default rows ship: periods are created when something needs them.

    The company has fiscalyear_lock_date, tax_lock_date, sale_lock_date, purchase_lock_date, hard_lock_date, stock_lock_date, and the internal counter fiscal_period_serial. The three specialized soft dates default to unset. Hard and stock dates move through their owning lock operations, never a generic company write.

    Creating periods: ensure_periods

    Section titled “Creating periods: ensure_periods”

    It creates the missing regular periods containing a day of the range for the company’s root and every branch, from the root’s fiscal calendar, and returns the number of rows created. Call it outside the unit of work that needs the periods (before its first statement, or after it failed for a missing period and before it is restarted): it runs in its own short transactions as caller (the settings read, then the insert at READ COMMITTED), so the new rows are visible to the restarted unit. It is idempotent; two concurrent calls for the same month both succeed. Statuses of new rows are derived from the company lock dates, the latest over the company and its ancestors. A date beyond the horizon setting, a range needing more than 1,200 periods, or a fiscal year end that no longer matches the existing periods is refused by name. ensure_periods_with takes a calendar read once with FiscalCalendar::read (or built with FiscalCalendar::new, which checks the horizon bounds), for imports that ensure many ranges.

    The fiscal calendar settings

    Section titled “The fiscal calendar settings”

    Company settings, block “Fiscal periods” of the Accounting settings page, read from the root company:

    KeyDefaultBounds
    account.fiscalyear_last_day311 to 31, and a day the month has (29 February accepted)
    account.fiscalyear_last_monthDecemberJanuary to December
    account.fiscal_period_horizon_months121 to 120

    Periods are one month long (ADR 0048 D3 R2). A fiscal year ending on the 15th has periods from the 16th to the 15th; a February end of 28 or 29 means the month end. Changing the fiscal year end after periods exist makes the next ensure_periods that would overlap them fail loudly.

    The day and the month are checked together on every settings write (the setting group account.fiscal_year_end): setting February while the day stays 31 is refused, naming both keys. A branch shows the block too but its values are not used; it follows its root company. Hiding the block at branch scope needs a scope the settings framework does not have yet.

    Before writing a dated row: period_admission

    Section titled “Before writing a dated row: period_admission”

    One verdict per (company, date): Open, NoPeriod (run ensure_periods, restart), FiscalLock (an accounting writer applies its re-dating policy, planned with posting; a stock writer refuses), HardLock (everyone refuses), StockLock (stock class only). Accounting writers ignore the stock status. The call takes the shared advisory lock (PERIOD_LOCK_CLASS, period_id) of each period first, so a lock operation’s T1 waits for in-flight writers and its drain covers the rest. Call it once per batch at the start of the writer’s unit of work. Sale, purchase and tax soft locks, exceptions and the backdating window are not period-row questions; the lock and posting work adds them.

    Status operations: status

    Section titled “Status operations: status”

    The status operations all require account.group_account_manager and take the exclusive advisory locks of the periods they may change, in id order.

    Who may act on which company (SPEC-ACCLOCK-104). Outside superuser mode every period operation, ensure_periods and period_admission act only on a company in the caller’s active companies (Transaction::companies) or on a branch below one: a branch follows its parent’s lock dates, as in the reference (odoo/addons/account/models/company.py:572-577). Anything else is refused with Access (“One of the selected companies does not exist or is not one of yours.”), also for a period or company id that does not exist, so the answer does not tell whether it exists. Superuser mode is not limited.

    What the synchronisation waits for (SPEC-ACCLOCK-105). sync_lock_dates first takes the exclusive advisory lock of every period whose status it may change, before its refusal checks and its updates. A writer that was admitted for such a period (it holds the shared lock) commits first; a writer that asks after the synchronisation committed sees the new status. The wait is bounded by the transaction’s lock_timeout (OrmConfig::timeouts, or BeginOptions::timeouts for the unit); the lock operations’ own SET LOCAL lock_timeout is planned. A writer whose transaction already read the period before the synchronisation committed (a repeatable-read snapshot) still sees the old status: for the hard lock the drain of the two-step run covers it; for the fiscal-year lock nothing does yet, and the case is left to the lock-date setters (planned). A synchronisation that would change more than 1,200 periods per company of the tree at once is refused; move the lock date in smaller steps.

    Same transaction, one lock order. sync_lock_dates flushes the transaction first, so a setter may write the lock date through the record API and call it right after. Every path takes its locks in one order: the target company row, then the root company row (fiscal_period_serial), then the period advisory locks in id order; the second step of a lock run takes the two company rows FOR UPDATE first, so a setter and a lock run on the same company wait for each other instead of deadlocking. That second step needs a READ COMMITTED transaction (BeginOptions::isolation(Isolation::ReadCommitted)); in a REPEATABLE READ one it is refused with Programming.

    • sync_lock_dates(tx, company_id): after a setter wrote a company lock date, closes and reopens periods to match, and reopens the stock status when the stock lock date was lowered. Refused, before writing, when the fiscal-year lock would reopen a hard-locked or a stock-locked period, or when a hard or stock lock date was moved past the periods its operation locked.
    • PeriodSet::of(tx, period_id): the period and the matching periods of its company’s branches.
    • hard_lock_begin / hard_lock_complete / hard_lock_reset and stock_lock_begin / stock_lock_complete / stock_lock_reset: T1 (returns a LockRun with the generations), the T2 write when the counts are zero (writes the company date with GREATEST and bumps the root’s serial), and the T2 refusal or audited cancel. Both locks run in order: T1 is refused while an earlier regular period of the company has not reached that lock. A T2 that matches no row returns LockOutcome::NotApplied with the period states (cancelled, superseded, or already locked by the hard lock).

    The caller owns the rest of each operation: SET LOCAL lock_timeout, committing T1, the drain, the counts T2 checks (drafts, unreconciled statement lines, readiness, replay jobs), and the close_period precondition for companies on the valuation bridge (ADR 0147 G4). Those are planned.

    Not yet: tax_return_id and the tax-return close, the adjustment periods’ creation, the database trigger that refuses writes to locked rows from raw SQL (it needs addon-declared SQL objects), and the stuck display of an interrupted lock.

    Accounting lock-policy foundation

    Section titled “Accounting lock-policy foundation”

    The native erp_addon_account::locking API provides LockCheck::new(company, date, JournalClass, TaxImpact) and check_lock_dates(tx, &checks). Construct inputs from authorized business records. The result preserves input order: LockVerdict::Allowed or a typed refusal. into_result() retains its precise error kind and message. Fiscal-year and hard dates bind every accounting journal; sales and purchase dates bind their journal classes only; tax dates bind report-affecting changes only. A cutoff is inclusive. Effective dates take the maximum over the company and its ancestors. A branch’s own date never restricts its parent. Hard-locking period rows bind checks before the company hard date advances. A period that is merely missing does not refuse a post: action_post creates the periods its entries need inside its own unit of work before the lock check (fiscal::ensure::ensure_for_rows, which checks the companies against the environment the post was authorised with). It creates only the periods that contain a posted date, for the root company and its tree, not the months between two dates. Two units that create the same new month at the same time: the second waits for the first one’s commit and then fails with a serialization failure. Inside a retried unit of work (Orm::run, as the RPC layer runs every call) the retry finds the period and posts; a caller that runs the unit itself without a retry sees that failure and posts again. A date beyond the horizon setting, a lock date and a closed or locked period still refuse. Other writers that ask period_admission directly still get NoPeriod for a missing period.

    Call once per batch in the same transaction that writes the records. State is private to the call; subsequent calls reread it, including after savepoint rollback. There is no process cache or reusable permission token. Entry and line hooks are still being integrated, so calling this function is not proof that every entry mutation already enforces it. Transaction-wide caching and user exceptions are not implemented.

    change_lock_dates(tx, company, &LockPatch::new().set(LockField::Sale, Some(date))) changes soft dates. An omitted field is unchanged; None clears it. Accounting-manager rights and company scope are required. Generic company writes use the same policy and support one company per lock-date patch. Company creation accepts only unset dates. Future dates are refused using the caller environment’s current-date helper, on every path that changes them (ADR 0206).

    A fiscal-year change calls sync_lock_dates and therefore respects hard/stock reopening refusals. Sale, purchase and tax changes do not close fiscal periods. Company dates, period changes and the typed change record in the audit trail commit together; audit failure rolls everything back. A no-op records no change event. The operation uses a savepoint, so a caught refusal does not leave partial dates or audit events.

    Hard activation is explicitly unavailable until the maintenance drain, ledger/bank checks, activation audit and applicable valuation-close checks are proven. The status primitives above are low-level building blocks, not a safe user-facing activation coordinator. Draft/unreconciled-line checks, exceptions, automatic date shifting and the lock-date wizard remain incomplete. Existing repeatable-read writers retain their snapshots; this foundation adds no soft-lock drain.

    The multi-company foundation verifies the native policy across root, branch, grandchild and sibling companies. A branch uses the maximum applicable cutoff from its ancestor chain; its own later cutoff does not restrict its parent or sibling. Diagnostics retain all applicable cutoffs in date order. These are native-policy guarantees only: the ACCMC tests remain partial until the entry owners integrate posting, reset and editing paths. User lock exceptions remain unimplemented under ADR 0206.
